How likely is a large loss from a wrong SSI?

Losses where money went to the wrong account because a standing settlement instruction was wrong: an honest error, or a fraudster changing the payee's bank details. The estimate is for one bank's market-operations payments to clients and counterparties. Amounts are in USD millions; at today's rates read them as roughly the same in EUR.

Events caused by wrong SSIs

Every public case of USD 1m or more found where the only failure was the payee's bank details. All are fraud: no honest SSI error with a loss of 1m or more was found in public sources.

Honest SSI errors

  • Very frequent, small: SWIFT estimated about 40 million SSI-related payment errors a year, costing the industry about USD 700m (about USD 18 per error, 2011 study). Incorrect SSIs cause about 20% of trade fails (DTCC study by TABB Group), the largest cause after lack of securities (FMSB, 2024).
  • Rarely extreme: a payment sent with a wrong SSI normally lands at a real bank and is recalled. Losses come from interest claims, overdrafts and repair work, not lost principal. No public case of a lost principal of 1m or more was found. With zero public cases across roughly 45,000 bank-years (about 3,000 banks over 15 years), the 95% upper bound is about 1 in 15,000 bank-years. The model uses 1 in 10,000 to allow for losses that were never published.

How the numbers are calculated

  1. Count large US email-payment frauds. The FBI received 21,442 business email compromise complaints in 2024. In a study of 7,925 FBI cases, 52.5% of attempts succeeded and 1.4% of successful thefts exceeded USD 1m. That gives about reported losses of 1m or more a year.
  2. Correct and narrow. Assume half of large losses are reported to the FBI, and half are payee-detail redirection rather than CEO fraud. Divide by about 40,000 US organisations that routinely make payments of 1m or more. The result is the corporate rate: about a year per large payer (range 0.35% to 0.54% under the low and high assumptions).
  3. Method A: adjust to your bank. Multiply by your control factor and your payment volume, then add the honest-error rate (1 in 10,000 years).
  4. Update with your history. Method A is a prior, treated as a Gamma distribution with shape 1 (wide uncertainty). Your own record of n losses in T years updates it: rate = (1 + n) / (1/prior + T). With no losses in 10 years the rate barely moves, which is correct for a rare event.
  5. Method B: your caught attempts. If you catch A fake SSI changes a year and your controls miss a share m, about A × m / (1 − m) succeed. Multiply by the share aimed at payments of 1m or more. This is bank-specific and usually the better guide once you have two or three years of attempt data.
  6. Loss size. Gross amounts above USD 1m follow a Pareto tail (alpha), capped at your largest payment. A random share is recovered (Beta distribution with your average). The page draws 200,000 losses to get the chance that the net loss passes 1m, 10m or 50m.
  7. Annual probability. With rate λ and per-event chance q, P(at least one in a year) = 1 − e−λq.

What is not counted

  • CEO fraud and fake instructions (Ubiquiti, Crelan, the Arup deepfake): the payee details were not the failure; someone posed as an executive or client.
  • SWIFT hacks (Bangladesh Bank and others): fraudulent payment orders, not wrong SSIs.
  • Amount and duplicate errors (Citibank and Revlon, Santander's Christmas payments): right account, wrong amount.
