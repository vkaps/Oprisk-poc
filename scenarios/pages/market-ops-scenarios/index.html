How to read it

  • Fit rates how close the event is to your business. High means the same product and process as your client, back-to-back or payments flow. Medium means the same validation at a different scale or product, or a front-office-led failure the back office can flag. Low means it needs a large trading book. Low-fit events are hidden unless you include them.
  • Where the exposure came from: client, interbank counterparty, back-to-back hedge, payments and nostro, own positions, or regulatory.
  • Severity for your model: own-position losses are capped by your small limits, but payment, settlement, client-default and capacity events scale with the size of a single payment or client relationship. They are the ones that set the extreme tail of your scenarios.
  • Internal fraud is flagged so it can be kept apart for a separate exercise. These events are still listed because the back-office validation that failed (confirmations, price verification, reconciliation) is the scenario.
  • Loss is what the firm lost or paid. Near misses show the exposure (amount sent or at risk), not a loss. USD at average rates of the year announced.
  • Gaps: few public cases exist for caps, floors and callable swaps, or for deposits and loans outside payment errors. Use the rates rows (DZ Bank, NatWest swaptions, Monte dei Paschi) as anchors for those scenarios.
  • Data: market_ops_bo.csv on GitHub, part of the full Extreme Loss Library.
